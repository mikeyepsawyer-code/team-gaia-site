SECTION 1 — CANVAS & FONTS

  Canvas 1950x2850 @ 300dpi. MARGIN=169 (top), FULL_TEXT_W=1650.
  Cinzel 700 Bold -- the two true capitals (I, W).
  Cormorant Garamond 400 (regular, upright) -- "am" and the "e" in "We"/
  "Me". Genuine lowercase letterforms -- Cinzel's own lowercase glyphs
  are shaped like small capitals, not real lowercase, and read wrong
  for this purpose (verified by direct side-by-side render comparison).
  Parisienne 400 -- the subtitle.
  Cormorant Garamond 400 Italic -- the author name.
  Chiseled gold only (apply_chiseled_gold, tone-sampled STATIC/PASTEL
  stops via gold_finish_for_tone). No shimmer/foil treatment on the
  static PRINT/default card -- see Section 5 for the separate animated-
  shimmer exploration, which is a distinct deliverable, not applied here.

SECTION 2 — TEXT ELEMENTS, TOP TO BOTTOM

  1. TITLE: "I am We" -- default title location (top_y = MARGIN = 169),
     default FULL_TEXT_W sizing. Only "I" and "W" are true full-height
     capitals; "am" and "We"'s "e" are genuine lowercase.
     - Cap point size: 400 (base render, before final width-fit scale).
     - Lowercase point size: cap_pt * 1.20 (NOT 0.70x -- point-size
       ratio is not the same as actual rendered cap-height ratio across
       different type families. Cinzel's cap-height is much larger
       relative to its own em/point-size than Cormorant's is. Verified
       empirically: 0.70x point size produced an actual pixel-height
       ratio of only ~40%; 1.20x point size is what actually yields the
       intended ~70% ratio. ALWAYS measure rendered ink heights directly
       when calibrating a cross-font size relationship -- don't trust
       point-size ratios alone.
     - Word order/spacing: "I" + gap + "am" + gap + "We" ("We" itself =
       W + tight letter-gap + e, kerned as one unit, NOT word-gap
       spacing). Word gap = cap_pt * 0.18 (tuned down from an initial
       0.35 -- felt too wide). Letter gap (W-to-its-own-e) = -6 *
       (cap_pt/320) scaling factor, essentially touching.
     - All four pieces (I, am, W, e) baseline-aligned by true font
       baseline (render_ink_baseline), NOT bottom-of-bbox -- none of
       these glyphs have descenders so the two methods mostly agree,
       but baseline alignment is the robust choice if that ever changes.
     - OPTICAL DROP ON W ONLY: Cinzel's W has sharp pointed feet (not a
       flat stroke touching the baseline), so it optically reads as
       floating above the line next to flat-bottomed letters like I.
       W (and ONLY W, not its own e -- confirmed explicitly with
       Michael, the e must stay on the true baseline) is nudged down by
       cap_pt * 0.028 to compensate.
     - Whole title composite cropped tight, scaled to FULL_TEXT_W,
       centered, pasted at top_y=169.

  2. "Me": We's own W (from the finished, final-scale title -- not a
     separately-sized piece) is vertically mirrored (ImageOps.flip --
     true top/bottom mirror, not a 180-degree rotation and not a
     horizontal mirror) straight down, ~1/8" gap between We's baseline
     and M's top. We's own "e" is copy-dragged straight down too,
     bottom-aligned to M (M has no descender, so M's own bottom = its
     baseline). The WHOLE "Me" (M + e together) is blurred as one unit:
     GaussianBlur radius ~13, built with generous transparent padding
     (radius x4) before blurring so the blur has room to bleed without
     hard-clipping at a crop edge, then composited over the sharp
     background. CRITICAL: never paste the sharp glyph to canvas first
     and then paste a blur on top -- the blur's alpha never reaches full
     opacity at its own center, so the sharp version shows through and
     the blur looks like barely anything happened. Build the blurred
     version from the rendered face directly and ONLY paste that.
     "We" itself stays completely sharp.

  3. SUBTITLE: "the light of being" -- ONE line (not split, no hyphen),
     Parisienne, standard scaled_gold_text call to FULL_TEXT_W.
     STANDING RULE (added Sep 6 2026, also added to
     COVER_PRODUCTION_STANDARD.txt Section 2B as a catalog-wide default):
     the subtitle sits directly above the author line, not under the
     title. Compute the author's position FIRST (see item 4), then
     probe-render the subtitle to know its height, then place it
     bottom-anchored a small gap (60px) above the author's top edge.
     This leaves a large open photo area between the title block and
     the subtitle -- intentional, not a bug to fix.

  4. AUTHOR "Michael Sawyer": Cormorant Garamond Italic, FULL_TEXT_W,
     chiseled gold. Bottom margin: 7/16" from the canvas/card edge to
     the author's COMMON bottom (common_ink_bounds, excluding the "y"
     descender) -- catalog-wide default as of Sep 6 2026, up from 5/16".
     The old 5/16" figure was calibrated back when author names mostly
     used all-caps fonts with no descenders; it doesn't leave enough
     room now that the default author font is mixed-case Cormorant
     Italic. See COVER_PRODUCTION_STANDARD.txt Section 9 for the full
     rule and reasoning. Always verify the FULL ink extent (including
     the actual descender), not just the common-bottom number, actually
     clears the edge with some margin -- don't just trust the formula.

SECTION 3 — THINGS ABANDONED THIS SESSION (worth knowing, not repeating)

  - "We are Me" with an attached script "are" or italic "refract as"/
    "refracting" word, band-anchored horizontally to the vertical grass
    line: built through ~15 iterations, ultimately abandoned as overly
    complex for a look Michael wasn't fully happy with ("this one's
    pissing me off"). The simpler "I am We" -> "Me" concept replaced it
    entirely and was reached in a couple of passes once tried.
  - A light patch (soft radial glow) pasted OVER an already-blurred Me
    to add "more light" -- doesn't work, just reads as more blur, since
    a translucent wash over something already low-contrast reduces
    contrast further rather than brightening it. Don't do this; if a
    blurred element needs to look brighter, adjust its own color/
    brightness before blurring, not via an overlay after.

SECTION 5 — ANIMATED GOLD SHIMMER (exploratory, NOT applied to this
cover yet -- separate deliverable, parked as of Sep 6 2026)

  Concept: an existing baked-video shimmer system (tools/bake_shimmer_
  blue.py, built for Shamanic Photography) sweeps highlight bands across
  text. Michael wants a gold "organic" variant with THREE highlight/
  lowlight color pairs, one per band of the light sweep, rather than one
  shared pair:
    Band 1 (brightest): butter yellow (255,225,140) / light green
      (210,225,175) -- pulled from the water in the Slow the Fuck Down
      painting.
    Band 2 (mid): soft orange (230,160,90) / burnt sienna (140,74,46)
      -- burnt sienna chosen over an initial copper idea; Michael isn't
      married to any specific bridge color, this was explicitly framed
      as "set up the structure and play with it."
    Band 3 (darkest): burnt sienna (140,74,46) / chocolate (60,38,24)
      -- burnt sienna bridges band 2 and band 3.

  Key technical direction (Michael's idea, and a good one): drive the
  color sweep off the CHISELED bevel's own per-pixel surface normal
  (nx, ny, nz from chisel_shade's distance-transform geometry) rather
  than a generic diagonal band overlay independent of the letterform.
  Precompute the normal field ONCE per glyph (expensive: distance
  transform + gradient + blur), then animate cheaply by just rotating
  the light direction per frame (a dot product) and mapping the
  resulting per-pixel shade value through the 5-stop gradient above.
  Working prototype built and confirmed promising (video: rendered a
  test loop of "We" in Cinzel sweeping through the full palette).

  STILL MISSING before this is a real deliverable:
   - Dappled/mottled noise texture within each band (real foil isn't a
     smooth gradient) -- the blue baker's multi-octave noise field needs
     adapting in.
   - Premultiplied-alpha glow/blur pass (the blue standard's fix for
     muddy edge color).
   - Transparent-alpha background (prototype used a solid navy fill for
     visibility only).
   - Actual baked WebM (alpha) + MP4 (matte fallback) pair, not a raw
     PNG-sequence-to-MP4 test render.
   - Decision on WHERE this gets used: card only for certain, maybe the
     ebook-specific cover version too, explicitly NOT the print cover.
     Revisit "things like that" (other ebook-vs-print distinctions) as
     publishing gets closer.

  Parked here Sep 6 2026 -- Michael asked to be reminded about this in
  a day or two.
