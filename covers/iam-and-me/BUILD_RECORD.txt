I AM & ME — COVER BUILD RECORD
Status: DRAFT — v16, pushed for visual review, not yet approved.

This replaces the earlier build record with the SIMPLE, DIRECT path to
the current approved layout. The early sessions on this cover went
through many exploratory rounds (custom crops, a baseline-flip bug on
the mirrored M, wrong-font e's, etc.) -- none of that history is repeated
here. This is just: here is the fastest correct way to build this cover
from scratch.

SECTION 0 — SOURCE PHOTO

  Gaia photos: ALWAYS check tools/gaia_orient.py first. Never trim, only
  rotate. Confirm orientation OBJECTIVELY (compare average "sky-score" —
  brightness + blue-dominance — of the left half vs right half of the
  candidate frame) rather than by eye. Do this even if the raw file's
  width/height already looks like the right shape -- phone photos from
  this camera frequently have no EXIF orientation tag, so the raw
  container's aspect ratio does NOT reliably indicate true capture
  orientation.

  Current source: a straightened re-shoot of the Gaia Ridge winter
  sunrise (Drumheller badlands), rotated ~1 degree by Michael before
  upload specifically to make a vertical brown grass band in the photo
  perfectly vertical. Confirmed sky-right with no further rotation
  needed. Archive any new source photo to
  covers/iam-and-me/source_photo_[name].jpg before building.

  FIT WITHOUT TRIMMING: this photo's aspect doesn't exactly match the
  1950x2850 canvas. Scale by height (the limiting dimension) so the
  entire photo appears with nothing cropped, and fill the resulting thin
  side margins (currently ~48px total) by extending the outermost
  column/row of pixels -- not a generative fill, not a crop.

  THE VERTICAL BAND: there's a strip of brown grass running the full
  height of the photo (through the ridge crevice and continuing as the
  foreground dirt track) that Michael uses as a hard alignment reference
  for the We/Me text. Locate it OBJECTIVELY each time the photo changes:
  find the vertical column with the lowest average brightness across a
  tall y-range (e.g. rows 100-2700, columns 350-600), confirm visually
  with a ruled overlay before trusting it. On the current (straightened)
  photo this lands at x=523.

SECTION 1 — CANVAS & FONTS (standard, unchanged)

  Canvas 1950x2850 @ 300dpi. MARGIN=169, FULL_TEXT_W=1650.
  Cinzel 700 Bold -- title, and the W/M caps in We/Me.
  Parisienne 400 -- the two subtitle lines, "refracting".
  Cormorant Garamond 400 (regular, upright) -- the lowercase "e" in
  We/Me. IMPORTANT: do not use Cinzel for this e. Cinzel's lowercase
  glyphs are shaped like small capitals (a small "E", structurally),
  not genuine lowercase letterforms. Compare the two directly if
  rebuilding from scratch and this isn't obvious.
  Cormorant Garamond 400 Italic -- author name, and the word "are".

  No shimmer/foil treatment anywhere on this cover. Chiseled gold only
  (apply_chiseled_gold with tone-sampled STATIC/PASTEL stops via
  gold_finish_for_tone) -- never GOLD_FOIL_STOPS.

SECTION 2 — TEXT ELEMENTS, TOP TO BOTTOM

  1. TITLE "I AM": Cinzel 700, target_width = FULL_TEXT_W * 0.95 (a 5%
     downsize from the original approved size), top_y = 169 (MARGIN).
     Fixed stops = STATIC_GOLD_FOIL_STOPS, treatment = "high_contrast".
     Keep these exact fixed values on future rebuilds rather than
     re-sampling tone from a new background -- this element is meant to
     be visually IDENTICAL across cover revisions.

  2. SUBTITLE, two short lines, right-shifted into the sky (not centered
     under the title): "the light" / "of be-ing" (the hyphen in "be-ing"
     is deliberate stylistic word-breaking, not a line-wrap hyphen).
     Base render: Parisienne 230pt, independently for each line (not
     width-matched to each other).
     Sizing history that nets the current size, applied as sequential
     multipliers to the raw 230pt render, in order:
       a) +75px added to each line's raw width (a quarter-inch upsize,
          split as 1/8" per side, symmetric around each line's own
          center)
       b) +10% uniform scale on top of (a), again around each line's
          own center
     Right edge: both lines share a right edge at
     (CANVAS_W - 190) + 37.5px (the 37.5px accounts for the symmetric
     growth in step (a) pushing the shared right edge outward).
     sub1_top_y = title_bottom + 55. sub2_top_y = sub1's common-bottom
     (via common_ink_bounds) + 35.
     Tone: gold_finish_for_tone sampled behind the subtitle region,
     treatment "chiseled".

  3. "We [e] are / Me [e]" -- the construction:
     - W: Cinzel 700 @ 320pt base, rendered alone.
     - M: the SAME W ink, ImageOps.flip() (a true vertical/top-bottom
       mirror -- NOT a 180-degree rotation, and NOT a horizontal
       mirror). Note: for this particular glyph in Cinzel, flip and
       180-rotation happen to look identical because Cinzel's W is
       already left-right symmetric -- but flip is the semantically
       correct operation and is what's specified, so use it directly
       rather than relying on that coincidence.
     - e: Cormorant Garamond regular @ 260pt (matches "are"'s point
       size). The SAME rendered e is reused for both We and Me.
     - are: Cormorant Garamond Italic @ 260pt.
     - ALIGNMENT: bottom-align everything (W, e, are, M, e again). None
       of these letters have descenders, so their true visual baseline
       IS their ink-bottom row. Do NOT try to compute M's "baseline" by
       transforming W's baseline row through the flip -- flipping the
       glyph also flips which end of the cropped image is top vs.
       bottom, so that math points at M's TOP, not its bottom. Bottom-
       alignment sidesteps the whole problem.
     - W/M share IDENTICAL x-position on the working canvas (not just
       "centered independently to look similar") -- since M is
       literally W's own ink flipped, same width, same x guarantees
       same center automatically.
     - Kerning: letter-to-e gap -6px; We-to-are gap 55px.
     - W-to-M vertical gap (W's bottom to M's top): 1/8" (37.5px) in
       final canvas pixels. Achieved by building the whole two-line
       composite once, measuring its native size, computing the scale
       factor needed, then converting the inch target back into base
       (pre-scale) pixels for a second build pass.
     - Overall pack size: pinned to a fixed final width of 1055px.
       This does NOT auto-derive from remaining vertical space -- it's
       a fixed value carried forward from the approved size. If the
       subtitle or other elements change size later, do not let that
       silently change this number.
     - Position: horizontally, the W/M shared x-center lands at
       BAND_X minus a 1/16" + 1/32" leftward nudge (accumulated over
       two rounds of adjustment -- net offset from the band's raw
       measured position is 3/32", about 28px at 300dpi). Vertically,
       the whole two-line pack is positioned so "Me"'s bottom lands
       exactly on "refracting"'s text line (see item 4) -- i.e. compute
       refracting's position FIRST, then derive the pack's top_y as
       (refracting's line y) - (pack's total height).
     - "Me" IS HEAVILY BLURRED (current version): after building and
       placing the pack normally (sharp), extract just the M+e pixels
       for the Me line (with alpha) from the finished pack, pad
       generously (radius x4) so the blur has room to bleed without
       clipping, apply GaussianBlur(radius=26), and paste that blurred
       piece back over the same spot on the canvas. This blurs only the
       gold letterform -- the sharp photo underneath is untouched.
       "We are" stays crisp. This is a deliberate thematic contrast
       (the narrative "Me" softening/dissolving against a crisp "We").

  4. "refracting": Parisienne, sized to match the subtitle's effective
     point size after its own upsizing (i.e. same final scale factor as
     subtitle line "the light"). Horizontally centered under the
     subtitle block's own center (not the page center), then shifted by
     the same leftward nudge applied to the We/Me pack (they move as one
     unit). Vertically positioned independently of the We/Me pack --
     the pack is what slides to meet IT, not the other way around.
     Current absolute vertical anchor: subtitle_block_bottom + 868px,
     then an additional 3/16" (56px) further up per the latest revision.

  5. AUTHOR "Michael Sawyer": Cormorant Garamond Italic, target_width =
     FULL_TEXT_W, chiseled gold, tone-sampled. Bottom margin: position
     so the COMMON bottom (via common_ink_bounds, i.e. excluding the "y"
     descender in Sawyer) sits 0.375" (112.5px) above the canvas bottom
     edge -- NOT 0.25", which let the y-descender clip past the card
     edge on an earlier version. Always verify the actual ink bottom
     (including the descender) clears the canvas edge with some margin,
     not just the common-bottom math. Then an additional 1/8" (37.5px)
     further up per the latest revision.

SECTION 3 — THINGS THAT WENT WRONG, WORTH NOT REPEATING

  - Don't improvise a custom crop on a Gaia photo before checking for
    tools/gaia_orient.py.
  - Don't trust a phone photo's raw width/height ratio as a proxy for
    "already correctly oriented" -- these files often lack a real EXIF
    orientation tag.
  - Don't assume a font's "lowercase" glyphs are genuine lowercase
    letterforms. Cinzel's aren't (they're small-caps-shaped).
  - When mirroring a glyph to build a second letter (W -> M), be
    precise about WHICH mirror (vertical flip, horizontal flip, 180
    rotation are three different operations that can coincidentally
    look similar on symmetric glyphs but are not interchangeable in
    general, and their baseline/bounding-box math is NOT interchangeable
    even when the visual result is).
  - When a person gives a size/position instruction as a delta ("upsize
    by 10%", "move up 1/8""), check whether it's meant to compound with
    a shared formula that ALSO drives some other element's size --
    coupling two elements' sizes through one convenience formula can
    silently resize something nobody asked to change.
