What is This Thing, Called Love?

The total number of minds in the universe is one. In fact, consciousness is a singularity, phasing within all beings.   Schrödinger

They say if you are widely interested, you will be widely regarded as interesting. Similarly, if we love broadly, we will be broadly loved.
Love comes in several flavors; and knowing that there are more types of love to cultivate, means that we can reframe more relationships as types of love, and therefor experience love more often.
The word love is very ambiguous, so let's clarify the types of love, and compare these with other ways of modeling reality.
To start with, I define all types of love as the cooperative overlapping of perspectives. When you and I share we-identity. Not exactly that two become one; more like we retain our individual identities, while simultaneously tapping into a shared perspective.
If we became fully one, we would no longer be individuals who could co-relate; while if we remained as individuals, there would be no transcendent experience of union.
To put a finer point on it, we should probably say reunion, because we aren't so much joining together anew; more like returning to an underlying condition of prior union. And not so much returning to, as reopening the circuit between the supreme wholeness of be-ing, and our dream of localized individuality.
Matter as we experience it, is an illusory construct, with great utility. But in reality, there are no things; the universe is a singular verb which refracts with internal verb relationships. It is this inter-relationality of verbs which produces the appearance of nouns. The appearance of matter is a co-projected cognitive construct.
If we were able to take a picture of the universe, we would find that it is a holographic inter-projection of particulate perspectives. Add in the dimension of time, and these perspectives become narratives. Each of these is a timeline dreamt by the supreme consciousness, within which the universe constructs itself.
I'm not a big fan of the word God, because it is even more vague and misleading than the word love. Maybe we can work on that a little bit too, but I think that it deserves its own book(s).
To come back down to earth, I like to map the types of love onto the image of a sailboat. This is just a model, so take it with a little salt, or build your own if you prefer. Conveniently, this sailboat image resonates pretty well with the psychological model OCEAN.

OCEAN

The unexamined life is not worth living.  Socrates

OCEAN is a simple, elegant, modern psychological model. It has proven to be a robust tool to describe the human psyche. OCEAN refers to five broad qualities:
Openness to new ideas and experiences
Conscientiousness
Extroversion
Agreeableness
Neuroticism
Each of these 5 root categories is composed of two main elements. For example a person's conscientiousness rating comes from a total of their industriousness and orderliness. It is outside the scope of this book to dive too deeply into the detials of this model, but If you happen to be a person, it is worth Googling OCEAN. We'll primarily be using the OCEAN model as a parallel to the forms of love.

Come Sail Away

It seems to me that there are three main masts of love. Three categories within which love emerges; three relationships which love enlivens. These are Maternal/Paternal, Faternal/Societal, Unfamilial/Erotic.
As well, there are two meta-states of love,  each of which is the totality of these three. Amoré results from their addition. Agapé is the root wholeness from which the three are derived.
Amoré is the interplay of localities within plurality, bringing forth meaning and value; agapé is the prior action of wholeness, projecting localized being, plurality, meaning, and value.

## The Keel: Family Love

Extending deep below the surface, the keel represents our connection to family – both blood relations and chosen family. This love runs deep and provides stability, preventing us from capsizing in strong winds. Like a keel, family love often operates unseen but is crucial for maintaining balance.

This aligns with Conscientiousness in OCEAN – our sense of duty, responsibility, and commitment to those closest to us. The deeper the keel, the more stable the vessel.

## The Sails: Romantic Love

Romantic love is like the sails – catching the wind, providing forward momentum, and sometimes requiring adjustment. Sometimes we need to reef the sails in stormy weather; other times, we can let them out fully to catch every breath of wind. Like sails, romantic love can be exhilarating but also requires skill to manage effectively.

This corresponds to Extraversion in OCEAN – our capacity to reach out and engage with others, to feel excitement and positive emotions.

## The Rigging: Friendship Love

The rigging represents our friendships – the complex network of relationships that help us harness the power of our sails. Some lines are thick and crucial, others thin but still important. All need maintenance and occasional replacement. Like rigging, friendships require regular attention and adjustment.

This maps to Agreeableness in OCEAN – our ability to maintain harmonious relationships with others, to be cooperative and compassionate.

## The Deck: Universal Love

The deck represents our capacity for universal love – our connection to humanity as a whole, to nature, to the divine if you're spiritually inclined. It's the platform from which we observe the horizon and chart our course. This broader love gives context and meaning to all other forms.

The Second Familial Mast: Fraternal/Social

But now a great thing in the street, seems any human nod;
Where shift in strange democracy, the million masks of God.  Chesterton

The second mast is brotherly/sisterly love between kin.
Although this relationship is most commonly referred to as fraternal, strictly speaking that refers only to the masculine, as frater is Latin for brother.
Technically, sisterly love is sororal, from the Latin soror, sister, but this word has mostly fallen out of use.
Differentiating these by sex brings up another question: what would we call the platonic relationship between brother and sister?
Birth order often has a large impact on psychology. Recently, my own sister pointed out that due to these differences, siblings might not understand each other's viewpoint at all, but still may form a united front as a team.
Often the firstborn male is concerned with family history, continuing the lineage, and the "faith of our fathers".
Commonly, the middle child is very independent minded; often a troublemaker. In order to carve out a space for themselves, they feel it necessary to push back against the first born, or more generally against tradition.
The youngest often tends to be more "happy go lucky", free spirited.
Siblings  are affiliated by filial duty to the parents, and by familial collaboration. We may compete with each other, but still have the instinct to protect the litter, the parents and the wider family.
Fraternal love, takes many forms besides brotherly/sisterly. It expands out to members of any inclusive social structure or group with a like-minded goal. The frat house is a fraternity of learners; and here we see the return of the feminine soror, as sorority.
Businesses are fraternities of industry; teams, clubs, and religions are all projects of fraternal orientation. Morality, justice, and politics, are all extended versions of the fraternal urge to tame the tribal instinct.

"If you only knew the magnificence of the 3, 6 and 9, then you would have a key to the universe."  attributed to Tesla

Fraternal love ripples outward in threes.
The first triad is the litter — siblings bound by blood and by the shared project of surviving the same parents. Three is the smallest number that makes a tribe of a bond: two can face each other, but three must negotiate, and negotiation is the seed of society.
Double it and you get six — the clan. Two families joined by marriage, the extended kin, the village. Here fraternal love learns to include people we didn't grow up with, on the strength of a promise.
Triple it and you get nine — the tribe of tribes. The team, the guild, the faith, the nation, and finally the species itself. Chesterton's million masks of God — the great thing in the street. At nine, the ripple has traveled so far from the litter that we forget it started there. But it did. Every act of civic decency is sibling instinct, worn smooth by distance.
Tesla was said to circle a building three times before entering. Whether or not he ever spoke of the key to the universe, the pattern he loved is the pattern fraternity follows: each ring three times wider than the last, the same wave moving through larger water.
All types of fraternal love are forms of Agreeableness.
So if a person has an underdeveloped fraternal instinct, that may show up as a lack of empathy within a certain setting. For example maybe a person is not great at familial relations at the level of the nuclear family or the extended family; or maybe they're not a good member of the tribe, the country, the species.
Having the rare opportunity of sentience, begs the question – why are we often such dicks to each other? More importantly, what can we do about it?
One answer: chose fraternity over tribalism, within as many social structures as we are able. At every ring of the ripple, the same choice returns.

The Prow Sails: Amoré and Agapé

Nothing can be, everything can only inter-be. Thich Nhat Hanh

If we imagine these three types of love as the RGB primary colours of light, then their sum is white. When we activate this blend of all three loves, the resultant full spectrum has the strength to take us beyond ourselves, and fund Extroversion. Extroversion is composed of assertion and enthusiasm, depending whether the love/attention is focused or broadcast.
And here, for a moment, the crow's nest comes into view — though we won't climb it just yet. If the three masts are the primary colours, then there must be a white light which the prism of individuation split into that spectrum in the first place. The Vedic name for that white light is Satchitananda, and we'll unpack the word when we reach the crow's nest. For now, hold this: amoré is the recombined beam — the three colours summed back into a focused white; agapé is the memory of the original white — the wholeness that was never actually divided. One is arrived at by addition; the other was there before the prism.
Agapé is an unfocused full-spectrum love; love without an object; love for its own sake; love without the need for reciprocity. Psychologically this maps with enthusiasm.
Amoré is focused full-spectrum love; a rich, intense beam of affection, usually focused on a life partner but sometimes on a religious, heroic, or aspirational figure. Amoré maps with assertion; we project and assert our love toward our partner.
Back to the sailboat: amoré and agapé, are akin to the jib and the spinnaker. If we run a line from the top of the fore-mast down to the prow, a jib sits inside of that line like a Pythagorean triangle. Located so far forward from the center of mass, a jib has the most leverage to steer the ship. So in this model a jib represents amoré, which is a singular focused loving intention, steering and asserting the direction of care and attention typically toward a partner in monogamy.
Some larger ships have multiple jibs, in which case we can imagine the first as Amoré, a monogamous love for one life partner. Next forward is monogamish, which could be thought of as a hall pass. Then the paramour, the extra-marital lover. and finally full Polyamory. Obviously these don't suit everyone.
As we get farther forward, there is more and more effect on the steering of the ship. Also notice that the focus becomes wider, which brings us to the final and widest love-sail, the spinnaker.
Finally, some boats designed for speed have a spinnaker. Think of the Australia Cup race and imagine the huge billowing sail which inflates out the front.
Agapé is an unstructured, unfocused love, or you could say its focus is the entire universe. Its emotion is enthusiasm. En theos means in spirit, radiant, non-localized.
Agapé is potentially the strongest love; it is ecstatic, beatific, transcendent, anahata. When agapé is in full bloom, the heart chakra is wide open; and like a spinnaker, it powerfully drags the rest of you along behind it like a ragdoll.
Ecstatic love revivifies the appearance of locality from static to ex-stasis; from noun to verb; matter to spirit. Ecstasy frees the local knower, from known action-forms.
Agapé/euphoria is the experience of balanced flow between beatific pressure and ecstatic release, between potential and manifestation, between source and gaia, wu ji and tai chi. Agapé is akin to the breast plate of righteousness.

The Crow's nest: Anahata

All compound forms are transitory.  Buddha

Anahata means "That which is unhurt." In order to understand this, we need a little background. I guess you could say that Anahata refers to one of the qualities of Satchitananda.
Satchitananda is composed of three words: Sat Chit Ananda. Sat means Thusness: the reality behind all appearances. Chit means consciousness, and Ananda means bliss.
Together, the meaning is that the Prime Mover, the cause of the causal web, the Supreme be-ing which brings forth space and time, the underlying field, has the nature of consciousness and bliss as foundational qualities.
This doesn't mean that we have to default to theism. It can be that consciousness or inter-relativity are fundamental qualities of be-ing. If that is so, all action-forms derive as conditions within this unconditioned be-ing, and so the natural relationship between the original wholeness and it's self-same expression within a corporal framework, is naturally of the quality of benevolence or love.
And now the circuit we opened in the first chapter finally closes. Remember how we defined love: the reopening of the circuit between the supreme wholeness of be-ing and our dream of localized individuality. Satchitananda is that wholeness — and the sailboat, it turns out, has been its self-portrait all along.
Sat, thusness, is the hull and the very ocean it floats on: the reality beneath all appearances, the ground that keeps us buoyant whether or not we ever name it.
Chit, consciousness, runs up the three masts. Every act of maternal shielding, every fraternal ripple outward through the rings of three, every erotic dissolving of the self-other membrane — each is consciousness knowing itself through another's eyes.
Ananda, bliss, fills the prow sails: the enthusiasm of agapé, the assertion of amoré, the wind that was blowing before we raised a single sail.
Earlier we imagined the three loves as primary colours summing to white. Now we can finally say what the white is. Agapé is not a fourth love standing beside the other three; it is the white the three colours were all along, before the prism of individuation split it. Every mast is Satchitananda refracted through locality.
To climb to the crow's nest — anahata, the unhurt — is simply to see the whole vessel at once, and to recognize that the boat, the ocean, and the sailor were never separate. Nothing up here needs mending, because nothing up here was ever broken.

How to Agapé or Not

This above all: to thine own self be true, and it must follow, as the night the day;
Thou canst not then be false to any man. Shakespeare

The Bard is pointing to something approximating the principle of proximal action. We have to inflate our proximal sphere of perception, perspective, and influence (our "self") before we can inflate others. More properly said, if we allow the inner supply to inflate us, we will have an overabundance that we may offer to others. Notice the order of events: expansion, then distribution.

Depression is your body saying "Fuck you, I don't want to hold up this avatar any more." Jim Carrey

This brings us to one of the ways that I like to model personality with only two dimensions: expansion/contraction, and transparency/opacity of the self/other membrane.
To convert OCEAN to my system, Openness and Agreeableness become transparency; Conscientiousness is opacity; Extroversion  is expansion; Neuroticism is contraction.
So these two dimensions give us four conditions:
Opaque-contractive becomes depression as a vacuum forms
Opaque-expansive leads to anger because we expand up against our limitation.
Transparent-contractive is fear because externality chases us inward.
Transparent-expansive is joy whereww the overabundant self is spred out to others.
One might argue that the optimal state might be a high degree of expansion to power en theos iasm and assertion, with just a bit of opacity to provide the screen against which enthusiasm plays out.
We are like a semi-permeable membrane, between something like spirit and matter; or verb-ness and noun-ness;  direct experience and cognition. With the right osmotic pressure, we can stand in agapé.
We can allow our expansive inner potential to be overabundant compared to our outer contraction into locality. Just a little bit is all it takes. Even a ratio of 51% inner inflation by verb-ness, to 49% outer structure by noun-ness, will position us with the right direction of flow.
And as we become seated in love rather than fear, our presence transmits that freedom to others..
But the appearance of differentiation into particular locality, self-other duality, is needed to underwrite noun-ness. If we go too far in the direction of light, spirit, verb; we lose the unknown-ness which is required to uphold assumed plurality. The appearance of limitation and individuation is needed as a screen for the light to play against.
In Vedic philosophy they say that everything is composed of three gunas or modes of being: rajas, tamas, satva
Roughly Speaking, tamas Is excessively contractive; It represents sloth, indolence, rotten food. Rajas is excessively expansive; as in anger, excessive passion, spicy food. Satva represents appropriate expansion, balance, clarity, wisdom, sweet & pure food.
Once we find a taste of satvict balance, and if we move without malice, without excess attachment; the universe has our back.
So how do we get there? The short answer is "we don't ". There is nowhere to go; we are already here. The secret is to be what we already are, without insisting that we are not.
Our thoughts, our speech, our writing, our action, all inscribe our stance on our proximal sphere (our "present" moment). Simultaneously this subscribes our future to their emotional equivalent. Depending on our attitude, we constantly throw bombs or balms ahead of us, for our future self to encounter.
As we focus on positivity, the universe reflects it back to us; but it isn't quite that simple.
Attachment is off balance, by grasping; but many religions react to this with detachment; which is off balance, by pushing away. The right balance is non-attachment; wu wei; effortless action; surrender to grace, flow.
Sri Ramakrishna said, we are bound with two chains; one iron and one gold. Use the gold one to break off the iron, then throw away the gold and be free.
In other words, within the framework of plurality, we should choose the noble over the base; good over evil. God over devil if we prefer a deistic model. However we can also transcend dualities, and orient as a lens between dualities and wholeness. Wholeness is what the universe listens for.
Love transcends boundaries; grace balances boundaries. When we do neither, we then tend to conflate self/other, with good/bad.  This sets up a belief of self=good, other=bad, which eliminates the opportunity for graceful interaction.
