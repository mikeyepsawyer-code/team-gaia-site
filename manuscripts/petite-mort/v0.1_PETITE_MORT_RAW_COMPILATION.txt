SOURCE 1 — Slow the Fuck Down, Chapter 14 — "The Zipless Fuck"

Erica Jong gave us the phrase in her 1973 novel Fear of Flying, and was never entirely forgiven for it. Her heroine, Isadora, daydreams of an encounter with a stranger on a train: no names, no histories, no questions, no guilt. The zippers fall away "as if by magic." What made it zipless, in Jong's telling, was not just the absence of clothing-fuss but the absence of everything that usually comes attached — power, ulterior motive, the quiet ledger of who owes whom. Nobody using anybody. She called it "as rare as a unicorn," and half-suspected it didn't exist at all.

I want to take her word somewhere she didn't take it.

For Jong, the zipless fuck was about anonymity — strangers precisely because strangers carry no baggage. My version keeps her freedom from agenda but throws out the anonymity. What she located in the absence of history, I locate in the fullness of presence. The zippers still come undone by themselves — but not because the two people are nothing to each other. Because, for the length of the encounter, they are nothing but each other.

And I hear a second meaning in her word, one she may not have intended. Zip-less: not only without zippers, but touch-less. A meeting conducted at a distance — feeling across the gap, no contact required. The clothes were never really the point. Neither, as we will see in the field version of this idea later, is the touch. Hold that thought; I will come back to it.

A sexual encounter completely free of narrative burden. No before, no after, no obligation, no score. Two people meeting in pure presence, in pure appetite, in the full intensity of desire without any of the furniture that desire usually drags with it.

No fumbling, no negotiation, no history and no future. Just this.

This is not a fantasy of irresponsibility. It is a fantasy of presence. What Jong was describing — what she wanted, what she was naming for the first time for a generation of women who had wanted it and had no word — was sex as a complete event. Not a transaction, not an investment, not a chapter in a story. An encounter entire in itself, the way a piece of music is entire in itself. It begins, it unfolds, it resolves, and it is over. And it was enough. It was more than enough. It was everything.

The erotic practitioner who has done the work — who has learned to slow down, to listen, to bring the quality of attention that Part I and Part II describe — is capable of this. Not because they have abandoned care but because they have learned to concentrate it entirely in the present moment. The past is not in the room. The future is not in the room. There is only this field, these bodies, this reorganization of the space between two people who have, briefly, remembered what they are.

The zipless fuck, done well, is an act of complete presence. It asks nothing of tomorrow. It brings everything to now.

If we are lucky, we realize it's all about love.
Even this. Especially this.

Erica Jong coined the phrase in 1973 to mean a casual, harmonious sexual encounter free from ulterior motives and power games. I want to narrow it to something more specific — and stranger.

A zipless fuck, in my usage, is an interaction of subtle energies that feels, while clothed, like naked physical intercourse. The physical contact, if any, is incidental. What is happening is not in the body. It is between two nervous systems that have come into resonant contact.

I have had two of these in my life. I offer them not as mysticism but as data.

The first was 1982. A woman in line ahead of me at a movie theatre. We were close — three feet — and as she turned her head and our eyes met, something happened that I can only describe as a network handshake. Our energy bodies established contact. Over the following minutes we moved toward a shared space that was private, complete, and as erotically vivid as anything that has happened to me in a bed. We never spoke. We parted with a smile when the line moved. The whole encounter lasted perhaps eight minutes.

The second was 1986, at the closing night of Expo 86 in Vancouver, in a crowd of three hundred thousand people. A woman and I were pressed together by the mass of people for the better part of an hour. Without words and without much visible movement, we arrived at a shared erotic space that felt more interior than physical — though the physical component was undeniable.

What makes a zipless encounter possible? I think two conditions are necessary.

Both parties need to be, in that moment, operating from the heart rather than the mind — not calculating or managing but genuinely open. The vocabulary for this differs by tradition: heart chakra, limbic attunement, resonance, primary process. The language matters less than the state.

And both parties need to be working from the pelvis energetically — attention down in the body, specifically in the sexual center, not in the analytical mind or in the defended belly.

When both of these conditions are met by both people simultaneously, contact is possible at a level that bypasses ordinary social process. The encounter happens faster than thought. It is recognized, not decided.

This is the outer edge of what polygasm practice is developing in you. You will not manufacture zipless encounters. But you may begin to recognize the conditions in which they become possible — and, on rare occasions, to step into them rather than walk past.

A word of honesty about all this, because the temptation to overclaim here is strong and the temptation to over-dismiss is just as strong. Let me sort what I actually know from what I believe from what I cannot prove.

What is established: orgasm lives in the brain, not the genitals. Women who report reaching orgasm through imagery alone, with no physical stimulation at all, show the real autonomic signatures of orgasm when they do it — raised blood pressure, heart rate, pupil dilation, pain-threshold changes — comparable to orgasm from genital stimulation (Whipple, Ogden & Komisaruk, Archives of Sexual Behavior, 1992). That is solid. But note its limit: it is self-generated. One nervous system producing its own state.

What I describe is not that. It is a loop. I send a pulse, and she reacts as though she felt it, and reflects a burst back; or she sends first and I answer. Two nervous systems taking turns, each responding to the other. Self-generated imagery doesn't account for that, and I won't pretend it does.

Much of the loop has a plausible mechanism, and I don't reach for anything exotic to explain it. Human beings unconsciously read and entrain to each other constantly — micro-expressions, the catch in a breath, a shift in posture, the dilation of a pupil. Mirror neurons, limbic resonance, the documented co-regulation of heart rate and breath between close partners. We are built to feel each other across a gap, through channels we never consciously notice. A great deal of "she felt what I sent" lives comfortably here, and that is not woo — it is the ordinary, astonishing sensitivity of two mammals in attunement.

And then there is the part I cannot source. My honest experience is that something passes between us beyond those known channels — a genuine communication at a distance, not fully accounted for by the micro-signals we can name. I can't prove that. It isn't disproven either; it simply sits past the edge of what the research currently reaches. So I offer it as lived data from one life, held as an open question — not collapsed into the imagery studies, which don't cover it, and not waved away as impossible, which would be its own dishonesty. Take my two stories as exactly that: stories, true to how they felt, offered with the authority of direct experience.
