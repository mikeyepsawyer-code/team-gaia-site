THE THESIS

"I" already imagines the perfect life for "me."

That's the whole problem this book is trying to solve, and the whole
solution it's trying to offer, in one line. The narrating self is
constantly drafting a better version of this moment — one where the
weather's different, the body's younger, the conversation's more
interesting, the ache is gone. It does this so continuously and so
invisibly that most people mistake the draft for the baseline, and
mistake their actual life for a rough draft of it.

Radical ecstasy is not an achievement. It is not a peak experience
reserved for retreats, drugs, orgasm, or the top of a mountain. It is
the everyday integration of the numinous and the proximal imminent —
the sacred, found not elsewhere but exactly here, in the thing that is
imminent: the next breath, the next step, the coffee going cold, the
particular quality of light on a particular Tuesday. Ecstasy, properly
understood, is not ex-stasis in the sense of leaving. It's ex-stasis in
the sense of stepping outside the narrating self's draft and finding
that the actual, un-improved moment was never lacking anything.

This book is the practicum for that stepping-outside, done not in
special conditions but in the ordinary ones. By the way. As long as
we're here.

CHAPTER SEED — By the Way

[CANDIDATE — placement and full content pending Michael's pass]

Working premise: the phrase "by the way" is usually a throat-clear
before something minor. This chapter takes it literally instead — the
way itself, the ordinary path from one place to another, is where the
book's whole claim gets tested. Not the destination. The way. By it.
Alongside it. In no hurry to leave it for something more important.

Candidate territory: the mundane commute, the walk to get the mail,
the domestic errand — the places consciousness usually idles in
autopilot, revealed as identical in kind (not degree) to the numinous
moments people actually notice. If ecstasy has to wait for the
extraordinary, it was never radical. It was just rare.

---

CHAPTER SEED — As Long as We're Here

[CANDIDATE — placement and full content pending Michael's pass]

Working premise: "as long as we're here" is the phrase people use to
justify a small extra effort inside a task they were already doing —
as long as we're at the store, as long as we're up. This chapter
reclaims it as a stance toward the whole of embodied life: as long as
we're here — incarnate, temporary, located in this particular body on
this particular day — what does full presence to that fact actually
ask of us?

Candidate territory: mortality not as a problem to solve but as the
thing that makes "here" mean something; presence as a practice rather
than a mood; the difference between waiting to leave and being
willing to stay.
