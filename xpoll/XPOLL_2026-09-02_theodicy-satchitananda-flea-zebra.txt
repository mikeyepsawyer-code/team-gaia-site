Satchitananda is not a claim that the field is nice. Being,
consciousness, and bliss as the ground of things is not a promise
about what happens on the surface. A wave crashing violently does
not mean the ocean isn't water.

Suffering is not evidence against the sacred. It is pressure finding
the only channel available to it. Predation, cruelty, and every
atrocity committed in the name of tribe or god are the narrative
self at maximum contraction, mistaking its own noun-ness for the
whole of what's real. This is not the universe failing to make good
on some promise. It is the valve, stuck closed, doing what a closed
valve does under pressure.

A theodicy that makes real agony feel smaller than it is has bought
its comfort dishonestly. What the ground offers is not an excuse for
the suffering but a claim about what sits underneath both the
suffering and whatever eventually comes out the other side of it
worth having endured -- the same ground, showing two different faces
at two different points in the wave. Not perfection arriving to end
the pain, but the recognition that the ground was never actually
lost, even at the worst of it.

Contempt for what is small is a symptom of insecurity, not a
function of scale. What is genuinely secure in what it is does not
need to diminish what isn't. Wisdom, wherever it actually develops,
tends to move toward tenderness for the small rather than away from
it -- which is a claim worth testing against every wisdom tradition
and every wise person actually encountered, not taken on faith.

Nothing here resolves the specific instance of suffering in front of
you. It isn't supposed to. It offers a ground to stand on while you
look directly at what won't resolve.
