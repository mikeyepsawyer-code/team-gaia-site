[CLAUDE -- drafted, generalized from the Ch.11D exchange]

Atman is Brahman, in the wrong hands, is the seed of megalomania. Every
tradition that teaches this identity has had to build guardrails against
people using it that way.

Ramana Maharshi spent a career warning students against seizing "I am
That" as a costume for the small self to wear, rather than as the small
self's own unclenching. That distinction matters more than it looks,
because the easy phrasing does real damage: to call it a dissolution of
the small self implies the small self is a mistake to be corrected, and
that is not the claim, and it is not even coherent inside this book's own
terms. Plurality of perspective is the play, not an error. Non-attachment
is not detachment. The valve does not ask the narrative self to leave the
stage; it asks the narrative self to stop performing solo, gripping the
script, defending the part as though the part were the only real thing
in the building. Same actor. Same stage. Same body carrying the same
hundred years. What changes is whether the performance knows it is a
performance.

So the test is never "did the self disappear." It is this:

Does the insight make you feel more separate from other people --
smarter, chosen, exempt? That is the narrative self smuggling itself back
in wearing nondual language, the closed valve pretending to be open.

Does it make the distinction between your vantage point and anyone
else's feel thinner, more like a technicality than a wall? That is the
realization working correctly, and it produces humility rather than
grandiosity, because it applies with zero exceptions. Everyone's "I" is
the same eye of I. That is not a hierarchy with you at the top. It is
the abolition of the hierarchy.

One more check, cheaper than the first two and just as reliable: can you
still laugh at yourself holding it? "Atman is Brahman, may as well own
it" is a sentence a person can rest in. "Hi, my name is Most High, Host
of Hosts" is the same sentence one costume-change later, and the fact
that it is funny is the tell. Finding it funny means you are still
standing in the local self, laughing at what it just recognized -- which
is exactly the deflation the whole test exists to produce. The joke is
not a failure to take the realization seriously. Taking it seriously and
finding it funny are the same gesture, viewed from two sides of the
valve.
