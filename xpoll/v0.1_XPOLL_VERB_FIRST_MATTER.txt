"We still act as if 'matter' is a thing. All of us put on this act on
the stage of life. Even scientists act as if materiality is a thing,
even though it's been well established for a century that there are
no things.

The universe is a holographic verb. A hyperlinked vision in God's
imagination.

The perspective of nounification, happens as a data-compression
utility within the flow of cognition.

As a system, we scan our proximal environment, convert the optical
qualities of photons into a pixelated electron stream. As that signal
passes from our eyes to the optic center at the back of our head, our
onboard software renders the data-stream via rastering and vectoring,
into a holographic experiential image.

We project that image outward to refract with all other perspectives.
This image making ability is process-heavy. Once we've made an image
of a room, we tend to keep a copy on file so that we don't have to
render the entire room next time we enter it. All we have to do is
compare our self-generated visual image against the fresh experiential
data and update only the parts of the hologram that have changed.

Every Planck-length event is a perspective within the matrix of
spacetime. There are no particles, only temporal particularities of
form. Log entries of charge and spin within the universal tome.

All compound forms are transitory. Every billion-year galactic
narrative, every sun, every planet, every civilization, every
earthworm, every coronavirus, is a compound verb.

And yet we all believe our own viewpoint to be so true that we call
that image, matter. Yes it matters to us, but we are just an
intermittent signal; prone to interference static; stumbling and
glitching our way across the stage of life.

The realization and held experience of this verb-first model, is the
beginning of wisdom.

So matter is a cognitive construct, red-shifted in time. Matter is a
historical record of an experiential reality. The physical body is a
historical remembrance of pain and trauma."
